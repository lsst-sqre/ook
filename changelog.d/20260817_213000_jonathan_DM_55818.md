### Bug fixes

- Stopped the intersphinx refresh job from re-downloading an inventory whose redirect chain it could not predict. A cached row records the terminal its content came from, and a `304` arriving from any other terminal was discarded and the whole chain re-walked unconditionally — correct, but charged to two cases that are not re-points at all. Every row cached before the `resolved_url` column existed reads as null, which was taken to mean "the terminal is the requested URL" rather than "the terminal was never recorded", so the first refresh of every redirecting inventory in the cache discarded a perfectly good `304`, re-walked the chain, and re-downloaded the body — and if the origin answered the second, validator-less request with another `304`, the row took a recorded failure and a backoff instead. An origin whose `Location` carries a per-response token or a load-balancer shard never lands on the same terminal twice, so it paid the same two chain walks and full body transfer out of a single fetch budget on every run, forever. The stored validators are now simply not sent anywhere but the terminal that minted them, so a moved or unpredictable terminal answers its one request with the body and a false `304` has no request to answer; a row with no recorded terminal sends them along the chain as before and records the terminal its `304` came from, so it is held to that terminal from the next refresh on. `If-Modified-Since` now also survives a redirect when the terminal that minted it is the one being asked, so a redirecting inventory with no `ETag` can revalidate at all.
